# Data model

SQLite through Drizzle ORM, one file on the Docker volume at `/data`. Migrations in `drizzle/` (0000_init through 0019_votes_comments) are applied at boot, with foreign-key enforcement off while they run and `PRAGMA foreign_key_check` after them (SQLite's recipe for rebuilding a table, which 0016 does to add constraints; `src/server/db/migrate.ts`). A database holding a row that breaks one of 0016's new constraints is refused with its old schema kept: a new CHECK fails inside the migration, which rolls back, and a row the two new foreign keys (`webhook_deliveries.audit_id`, `judge_invites.created_by`) would leave dangling is looked for before 0016 runs; 0012_triggers makes the audit-log, score-range and published-results triggers, and right after the migrations they are re-asserted from `src/server/db/triggers.ts`. Published results are final in the database too: `normalization_runs` and `normalized_scores` reject every UPDATE and DELETE; once an event's `results_published_at` is set, UPDATE and DELETE on its `scores`, `score_items`, `score_comments`, `comparisons`, `assignments`, `rubric_criteria` (the criteria and their weights) and `judge_overrides` are rejected, so is a change of a project's `track_id` or `duplicate_of`, and neither `results_published_at` nor the published run (`settings.publishedRunId`) can change. New rows are refused too (`0017_published_inserts`): a BEFORE INSERT trigger on each of those seven tables and on `normalization_runs` and `normalized_scores` aborts a row for a published event. The boot import inserts with ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING, which fires BEFORE INSERT triggers even for the rows it skips, so a row the table already holds by one of its unique keys passes: re-importing adds nothing and raises nothing. What the app still writes after publishing (signed records, the audit log, sessions, the outbox, webhooks, comments) is on none of these tables. All timestamps are ISO 8601 text in UTC, and most carry a `julianday(...) is not null` CHECK (or `is null or ...` for an optional one) so a malformed date cannot be stored; 0016_constraints added it to every timestamp of `events`, `scores`, `judge_invites`, `judge_overrides`, `voters`, `comments`, `api_tokens`, `normalization_runs` and `webhook_deliveries`. Where a row records who did something and when (`judge_invites.accepted_by`, `judge_overrides.revoked_by`, `voters.voided_by`, `comments.hidden_by`), a CHECK keeps the two set together or both null. Ids are text: rows created in the app get a prefixed random string (`usr_`, `prj_`, `evt_`, ...), while rows imported from the organizers' fixture keep the fixture's ids (`evt_01`, `trk_03`, `prj_32`); imported team members, who have no id in the fixture, get `usr_` and a hash of their email, so a second import finds the same row. JSON columns hold settings, lists and documents (event settings, gallery and tag lists, rubric anchors, run parameters, audit snapshots, webhook payloads, signed envelopes); every relation is a real table. Enum-like and range columns carry real CHECK constraints (Drizzle's TypeScript-only enums are not trusted).

## People and sessions

**`users`** — one row per person. `id` text pk; `email` text unique, stored lowercased (CHECK: equals its lowercase and contains `@`); `name` text; `password_hash` text, null for imported people who have no password yet (argon2id hash, never the password); `is_admin` int boolean, default false (true for an address in `ADMIN_EMAILS` that signed up through the one-time setup link, and for the demo organizer; administrators create and import events); `created_at` text.

**`user_roles`** — one role of one person in one event. Roles are rows, not a column, so a judge who is also a team member is one user with two rows. `user_id`, `event_id`, `role` (`organizer` | `judge` | `participant`), `created_at`; pk (`user_id`, `event_id`, `role`).

**`sessions`** — one signed-in browser or checker session. `token_hash` text pk — the SHA-256 of the cookie token; the token itself is never stored; `user_id`; `kind` (`login` | `checker`); `label` (checker sessions only: `organizer`, `judge_a`, `judge_b`, `participant`; CHECK: a label exists exactly when the kind is `checker`, and it is unique); `created_at`; `expires_at`.

## Events

**`events`** — one event. `id`; `slug` unique (CHECK: lowercase letters, digits, hyphens); `name`; `description`; `submissions_open_at` (null: open from the start); `submissions_close_at`; `judging_close_at`; `voting_open_at`; `voting_close_at` (CHECK: voting opens before it closes; publishing the results ends the vote: an open window closes at the publishing moment, one not yet open is cleared); `results_published_at`; `settings` json (skin, `reviewsPerProject`, `maxTeamSize`, `certificatePlaces` (places per track that earn a certificate of achievement, absent meaning 3; fixed once results are published), `judgingMode` (`scores` | `pairwise`, absent means scores), the published run, the organizer's not-duplicate pairs, the voting configuration: who may vote, favourites per voter, the open link's hash, `countLink`, whether open-link ballots add to the result, absent meaning no, and `linkPerAddress`, new open-link ballots per network address per hour, absent meaning 8; `voteRuleChanges`, each change to who may vote or the favourites per voter made after the first ballot, with when, why and the rules before and after; `voteCountChanges`, each duplicate merge or unmerge made after the voting window closed that moved the count, with when, the two copies and each project's votes before and after); `created_at`. CHECK: submissions open before they close.

**`tracks`** — one track of an event. `id`; `event_id`; `name` (unique per event); `position`. (`id`, `event_id`) is also unique — it is the target of the composite foreign keys that keep a team, project or assignment inside its own event.

**`prizes`** — one prize of an event. `id`; `event_id`; `name`; `description`; `position`.

**`custom_questions`** — one extra question the organizer adds for projects. `id`; `event_id`; `label`; `help`; `type` (`text` | `longtext` | `url`); `required`; `position`. The `fixtures.json` export carries an event's questions as `questions` and each project's answers as `answers` (by question id), and the import restores them, held to the Questions tab's limits (`src/server/project-limits.ts`: at most 20 with those the event has, a label of 3 to 200 characters, help of at most 300; past one, 422 naming the row), renaming a question id another event already holds. Answers come in only with a project the import creates: a project that is already here keeps its team's own, and the report lists each answer the file gives it that differs.

**`project_fields`** — what teams fill in: the organizer's choice for one built-in field of the project form. `event_id`, `field` (pk); `field` (CHECK: `title`, `summary`, `trackId`, `description`, `repoUrl`, `videoUrl`, `liveUrl`, `thumbnailUrl`, `galleryUrls`, `tags`, the form's own names); `mode` (CHECK: `required` | `optional` | `hidden`; a second CHECK: the track is never `optional`). No row means the default, which is how the form always behaved: title, summary and track required, the rest optional, so an event from before this table, or one nobody changed, needs no rows. A required title or track is checked on every save, the other required fields on submit; an optional title left empty is stored as the team's name, and so is the title of a project first saved while the title is hidden; while the title is hidden every project is shown under its team's name (a title typed before stays stored, and shows again when the title is asked again; duplicate matching, the `fixtures.json` export, `projects.csv`'s `title` and `scores.csv`'s `project_title` use the stored one; both CSVs give the shown one too, as a last column `shown_title`); a hidden track is the event's one track, and holds only while the event has one track (with a second one the track is asked again, and with one track again it is hidden again: a save stores only the fields it changes, so the organizer's choice is not overwritten by the mode shown meanwhile). A hidden field is ignored when sent, keeps what is stored, and is empty wherever the project is shown to anyone but its team and the organizers' exports. The `fixtures.json` export carries the choices that differ from the defaults as `project_fields`, and the import restores them (insert or ignore, like every imported row).

## Teams and projects

**`teams`** — one team. `id`; `event_id`; `name` (display only, may repeat; its members rename it while submissions are open, an organizer with an audited reason until results are published); `invite_code` unique, the code in `/join/<code>` — rotating it revokes old links; `created_at`. A team is deleted only when its only member dissolves it while submissions are open, and only with no project or a draft (the draft and its `custom_answers` go with it); the `team.dissolved` audit row keeps its name and the draft's id and title.

**`team_members`** — one person's membership. `team_id`, `user_id` (pk); `event_id`; `role` (`captain` | `member`); `joined_at`. The team changes its own rows while submissions are open; after that only an organizer adds or removes a row, with an audited reason, until results are published (certificates go to the members then). A composite foreign key (`team_id`, `event_id`) → `teams` pins the row to its own event, and a unique index on (`event_id`, `user_id`) enforces one team per person per event in the database, not only the app.

**`projects`** — one project. `id`; `event_id`; `team_id`; `track_id` (both composite-pinned to the event); `title` (non-empty; the team's name when the team gives none; shown as the team's name while the organizer hides the title, see `project_fields`); `summary`; `description`; `repo_url`, `video_url`, `live_url` (web addresses only: the data access layer and the import refuse anything but http and https); `thumbnail_url` (the same, or `/uploads/<name>` for a picture uploaded here, a file in the data volume; the export writes that one as a full address, so the import's web-address rule still holds); `gallery_urls` json array of at most 6 images in the order the project page shows them, each a web address or the `/uploads/<name>` of an image uploaded to this gallery, checked and exported the same way; once the project exists it changes only through the gallery's own route (an upload, or a new list, checked against the list the page last saw whenever the request names it, as the portal's own page always does) and an organizer's take-down, never with an edit of the project, so a stale edit cannot drop an image; `tags` json array of at most 8 tech tags of up to 40 characters, kept as typed but distinct without regard to case; `status` (`draft` | `submitted`; CHECK: a submitted project has a `submitted_at`); `duplicate_of` — set by the organizer's audited duplicate merge, must differ from `id`, and points to a project in the same event; the row itself is never deleted; `created_at`; `updated_at`.

**`custom_answers`** — one answer to one custom question. `project_id`, `question_id` (pk); `value`.

## Judging

**`rubric_criteria`** — one weighted criterion of an event's rubric. `id`; `event_id`; `key` (unique per event); `label`; `prompt`; `weight` real > 0; `scale_min`, `scale_max` (0 ≤ min < max ≤ 100); `anchors` json, anchor text per level; `position`. Once a score exists the set of criteria is fixed; a weight change then needs a reason and is kept in `events.settings.weightChanges` (when, why, the weights before and after), which the published results list. The `fixtures.json` export carries the rubric as `rubric` (key, label, prompt, weight, anchors, in order) whenever it differs from what the scores' keys alone would give, and the import takes it (insert or ignore), so an event moved to another portal keeps its weights and texts. Into an event that is already here an import keeps to the same rule as the Rubric tab: once a score exists, a file that would add a criterion (in its `rubric` or through its reviews' scores) or whose `rubric` leaves one out is refused whole (409 `rubric_in_use`, the tab's words); before the first score it may add criteria, each named in its audit row. Every import, into a new event too, holds the criteria it adds to the Rubric tab's limits (one shared constant each in `src/server/rubric-defaults.ts`), so the tab can save the rubric again: at most 16 with those the event has, a label of 2 to 60 characters (also the one a score's key gives), a prompt of at most 200, no two with one label; a file past one is refused whole with a 422 naming the row or the key. Labels, prompts and weights already here stay the event's own (the report lists each one the file gives differently), and an imported review counts as finished only with a score for every criterion the event has.

**`judge_tracks`** — one judge's claim on one track. `judge_user_id`, `event_id`, `track_id` (pk); the track is composite-pinned to the event.

**`judge_invites`** — an organizer's invitation link for a judge. `id`; `event_id`; `code_hash` unique — only the SHA-256 of the link's code, so the link is shown once; `name`; `email` (optional, lowercased); `track_ids` json array; `created_at`; `created_by` (references `users`); `accepted_at`; `accepted_by` (set exactly when `accepted_at` is); `revoked_at` (CHECK: an invite is accepted or revoked, never both). An address holds at most one open invitation per event: a new one for it revokes the open one, audited as `judge.invite_revoke` with `replacedBy` (the new invitation's id), and the old link then answers that a newer invitation replaced it (410 `invite_replaced`) rather than that it does not exist.

**`assignment_runs`** — one stored run of the assignment engine, so any assignment can be traced to its parameters. `id`; `event_id`; `mode` (`fixture` | `fresh` | `topup`); `seed` int; `params` json; `created_at`; `created_by`.

**`assignments`** — one project assigned to one judge. `id`; `event_id`; `judge_user_id`; `project_id` (composite-pinned); `run_id`; `batch_no` ≥ 1; `position` (the judge's seeded review order in the batch); `status` (`pending` | `done` | `recused`); `created_at`. Unique on (`judge_user_id`, `project_id`): a judge never gets a project twice. A row is deleted only while nobody has saved anything on it (no `scores` row, and in pairwise mode no answer by its judge about its project that is not taken back, since those answers are the judge's work there): an organizer taking it back, moving its project to a track its judge does not judge, or removing its judge; each is audited with the row's id (`assignment.remove`, `project.track_moved`, `judge.remove`), and runs treat a taken-back pair as a conflict from then on.

**`scores`** — one judge's score for one assignment; judge and project come from the assignment and are not stored twice. `id`; `assignment_id` unique (1:1); `submitted_at` — set only when every criterion is scored, a partial review stays null; `updated_at`; `conflicted` boolean — an imported score whose judge is a member of the scored project's team.

**`score_items`** — one criterion's value within a score. `score_id`, `criterion_id` (pk); `value` int. A CHECK cannot see another table, so the value's range (inside its criterion's `scale_min`..`scale_max`) is enforced by two BEFORE INSERT/UPDATE triggers that read `rubric_criteria`.

**`score_comments`** — the review text attached to a score. `score_id` pk; `feedback` (shown to the team after results are published); `private_note` (never shown to the team; organizers read it on the project's receipt on the results page and in `event.json`). Each save that changes a text writes the edit it made into its audit row (`feedbackEdit` / `privateNoteEdit`: `at`, `removed`, `added`, beside the lengths), so a rewrite leaves what the text said in the log, while typing adds only the new characters to each row; undoing the edits from the text as it stands gives back every earlier version (`src/lib/text-edit.ts`). The log is read by the event's organizers and the portal's administrators, who may read both texts; a webhook gets none of a review's values.

**`judge_overrides`** — the organizer's audited decision to include a flagged judge or exclude an unflagged one. `id`; `event_id`; `judge_user_id`; `mode` (`include` | `exclude`); `reason` (at least 3 characters once trimmed); `created_at`; `created_by`; `revoked_at`; `revoked_by` (either override can be undone; CHECK: `revoked_by` is set exactly when `revoked_at` is). Removing a judge who saved anything writes an exclusion whose reason starts "Removed as a judge: "; it cannot be undone while that person holds no judge role in the event.

**`normalization_runs`** — one run of the normalization engine, or of the pairwise fit. `id`; `event_id`; `method` (CHECK: `leniency-shrunk-v1` for a rubric run, `bradley-terry-v1` for a pairwise run); `params` json (W, β̂², σ̂², k, the judges left out, the flat-judge flags, the overrides with their reasons, the merges, each counted judge's n and leniency, and the rest of the engine's table that `normalized_scores` does not hold, so `normalized.csv` after publishing is read from the run itself; and in both kinds of run `trackMoves`: every project an organizer moved to another track, from and to with the track names as they were, the reason and when, which the public results show next to the project); `computed_at`; `computed_by`.

**`normalized_scores`** — one project's result within one run. `run_id`, `project_id` (pk); `n`; `raw_mean`; `normalized_mean`; `se`, one standard error of the normalized mean (null in runs stored before it existed); `rank_raw`; `rank_normalized` (reals, null where not computable). In a pairwise run the same columns hold: `n`, how many judges compared the project; `raw_mean`, the plain share of comparisons it won (ties half); `normalized_mean`, its win % against the track's average; `se`, that win %'s standard error; the ranks by each.

**`comparisons`** — one pairwise answer: which of two projects of one track a judge found better. `id`; `event_id`; `judge_user_id`; `track_id` (composite-pinned); `left_project_id`, `right_project_id` (composite-pinned to the event's projects; CHECK: two different projects); `new_project_id`, the project being placed (CHECK: one of the two shown); `outcome` (`left` | `right` | `tie`); `created_at`; `voided_at`, set when the judge takes the answer back (CHECK: ISO timestamps). The data layer only ever sets `voided_at` and never deletes a row, so every answer ever given stays readable (`comparisons.csv`). A partial unique index keeps at most one live answer per judge and pair of sides.

## Community vote and comments

**`voters`** — one voter in one event, of one of the organizer's chosen kinds. `id`; `event_id`; `kind` (`account` | `listed` | `link`); `user_id` (account voters); `email` (listed voters); `token_hash` unique — the personal or open link's token, only as its SHA-256; `order_seed` int, seeds the voter's own shuffled ballot order; `ip_hash`, `agent_hash` — salted SHA-256, only to flag suspected duplicate voters, never to identify; `created_at`; `last_voted_at`; `voided_at`, `voided_by`, `void_reason` (a void needs a reason of at least 3 characters, and `voided_by` is set exactly when `voided_at` is). CHECK: each kind carries the identity it needs (account → user, listed → email + token, link → token). A user or email votes once per event (unique indexes).

**`votes`** — one ballot entry. `voter_id`, `project_id` (pk); `created_at`. A ballot changes only while voting is open, by taking its picks off and putting the new ones on, and setting a ballot aside changes its voter row, not its picks. Triggers hold that (`0019_votes_comments`): an UPDATE of a pick is refused, and once the event's `voting_close_at` has passed (by the database's clock) or its results are published, so is a pick going on or coming off, so the public count is final in the database too.

**`comments`** — one signed-in person's comment on a project. `id`; `event_id`; `project_id`; `user_id`; `body` (1 to 2,000 characters once trimmed); `created_at`; `hidden_at`, `hidden_by`, `hidden_reason` — an organizer's hide, whose reason stays in place for the event's organizers and the comment's author (everyone else is not sent the hidden comment at all, so its author's name and the reason stay out of public view; CHECK: `hidden_by` is set exactly when `hidden_at` is); unhiding clears all three (the `comment.unhide` audit row keeps the reason). Its author can delete a row that is not hidden; the `comment.deleted` audit row keeps its id and length, not its words. Triggers hold what the app never does (`0019_votes_comments`): a change of `body`, `event_id`, `project_id`, `user_id` or `created_at` is refused, and so is deleting a hidden row.

## Signed records and keys

**`signing_keys`** — the portal's Ed25519 signing key, made at first boot. `id`; `public_jwk` json, served at `/.well-known/dogfood-keys.json`; `private_pkcs8` text, sealed: AES-256-GCM under a key derived from `DOGFOOD_SEED_SECRET` and the key's id (`sealed-v1:` and the base64 of nonce, tag and ciphertext), so with your own `DOGFOOD_SEED_SECRET` a copy of the database alone cannot sign (under the documented default anyone can derive the sealing key, so the portal runs on it only as the local demo, `SEED_CHECKER_SESSIONS=true` on a local address, and refuses to start otherwise); `created_at`. The private half is the one secret the database must keep: signing happens on demand, and the key is the root of trust for every certificate and record. Back up the database (`docs/OPERATIONS.md`, "Backup and restore") and keep the same `DOGFOOD_SEED_SECRET`, or records can no longer be issued under the same identity: under another secret the key cannot be opened, and the next start makes a new one (audited; records signed before still verify against the old public key, which stays published).

**`signed_records`** — one signed record per person, event and kind (a judge's participation record, or a team member's certificate). `id`; `event_id`; `kind` (`judge` | `participant`); `user_id`; `key_id`; `envelope` json — the record exactly as signed plus the base64url Ed25519 signature over its canonical JSON; `issued_at`. Unique on (`event_id`, `kind`, `user_id`).

## Webhooks

**`webhooks`** — one organizer's subscription: audited actions of one event, POSTed to a URL. `id`; `event_id`; `url` (CHECK: http or https); `secret` — the webhook's own secret, stored so each delivery can be signed `Dogfood-Signature: t=…,v1=<HMAC-SHA256>`; `actions` json (`["*"]` for all); `created_at`; `created_by`; `disabled_at`.

**`webhook_deliveries`** — the outbox and delivery log; a row is written in the same transaction as the change it announces, so none is lost or invented. `id`; `webhook_id`; `audit_id` (the audit row it came from; a foreign key to `audit_log`, which never writes to that table, so its append-only triggers are untouched); `action`; `payload` json; `status` (`pending` | `delivered` | `failed`); `attempts` — how many times it was sent: the worker tries six times with backoff (`MAX_ATTEMPTS` in `src/server/webhooks.ts`) and then marks it `failed`; each retry an organizer asks for sends it once more, up to 20 attempts in all (CHECK: 0 to 20; `ATTEMPTS_CAP`), after which a retry is refused with 409 `attempts_exhausted`; `next_attempt_at`; `last_attempt_at`; `response_status`; `response_body`; `error`; `created_at`; `delivered_at`.

## Email

**`outbox`** — one row per message the portal mailed or tried to mail (with `SMTP_URL` unset nothing is sent or recorded); the link in it is blanked, so no working key is kept. The row is written, audited, before the message is handed to the mail server, and a second audited write sets the outcome, so no message goes out unrecorded. An address the mail server would never be offered (not one plain address by the screens' rule, such as `ana@localhost`) is recorded in the first write as `failed` with its reason. `id`; `event_id` (null for the portal's own mail: password resets, administrator setup); `kind` (`judge_invite` | `voter_link` | `password_reset` | `claim_link` | `judge_reminder` | `admin_setup`); `to_email` (CHECK: holds an @); `subject` (1–200 characters; a longer one, from a long imported event name, is cut to fit with an ellipsis, in the mail too); `body` (1–20,000 characters); `status` (`sending`: recorded, not yet answered, and left so if the portal stopped mid-send; the outbox shows it as sending for 60 seconds, the longest a batch and its last send can take, and after that as no answer recorded, counted with the ones that may have arrived | `sent` | `failed`: it did not go out | `unknown`: the connection broke after the message may have been handed over, so it may have arrived; `off` is allowed but never written); `error`; `created_by` (null: the system, at start); `created_at`; `sent_at` (CHECK: set exactly when `status` is `sent`). An event's organizers read its rows; administrators read the portal's.

## API tokens and account claims

**`api_tokens`** — one person's named Bearer token for scripts; it acts as its owner with the owner's permissions and cannot make or revoke tokens. `id`; `user_id`; `name` (1–60 characters); `token_hash` unique — only the SHA-256, the token is shown once; `hint` (the first characters, to tell tokens apart); `created_at`; `expires_at` (null or after `created_at`); `last_used_at`; `revoked_at`.

**`account_claims`** — a one-time personal link for an imported person to set a password. `token_hash` pk — only its SHA-256, shown once; `user_id`; `event_id`; `created_by`; `created_at`; `expires_at` (after `created_at`); `used_at`. One unused claim per person: a new link replaces the old one.

**`password_resets`** — a one-time link an administrator makes for someone who lost their password. `token_hash` pk — only its SHA-256, shown once; `user_id`; `created_by` (the administrator); `created_at`; `expires_at` (a day later; CHECK: after `created_at`); `used_at`. One unused link per account: a new one replaces it. Setting the new password deletes the account's login sessions; the four demo identities are refused. Demo mode off deletes unused links a demo identity made.

## Audit log

**`audit_log`** — one row per change and per 403 refusal of a known person (a signed-in account, or a voter holding a link; at most 60 refusals per person in 10 minutes, then 429 and no row), written in the same synchronous transaction as the change it records. A request with no valid session (401) writes no row. `id` integer autoincrement; `at`; `actor_user_id` (null: the system — boot, import); `actor_label`; `action`; `event_id`; `target_type`; `target_id`; `before`, `after` json snapshots; `prev_hash`; `hash`; `salt` (32 random bytes as 64 lowercase hex, CHECK: that format or null; migration `0014_audit_salt`), only on the rows of the actions whose values a webhook leaves out (`vote.cast`, `review.save`, `review.submit`, `review.amend`, `pairwise.pick`, `pairwise.undo`, `fixtures.import`; `SEALED` in `src/server/webhooks.ts`), null on every other row and on every row written before the column existed.

Append-only by construction: two triggers reject every UPDATE and DELETE with `RAISE(ABORT)` (`0012_triggers`), and three hold the chain on INSERT (`0018_audit_chain`): a new row must link to the last one (its `prev_hash` is the last row's `hash`, 64 zeros before the first row), may not carry an `id` or a `hash` a row already has, and may not go before the last row by an explicit `id`. The second closes what the first two leave open on a connection without `recursive_triggers` (the portal's own turns it on; a plain `sqlite3` shell does not): there an `INSERT OR REPLACE` deletes the row it replaces without firing any DELETE trigger. SQLite has no SHA-256, so the database checks the links, not the hashes: a row with a wrong hash still goes in, and shows only when the chain is recomputed (below). The migrations make these triggers; they are re-asserted at every boot, and a trigger whose SQL was replaced (a no-op) is dropped and restored.

The chain: each row's `hash` is `sha256(prev_hash + "\n" + canonical JSON of the row's other fields)`, starting from `prev_hash` = 64 zeros. The canonical JSON is the object `{at, actorUserId, actorLabel, action, eventId, targetType, targetId, before, after}` (a missing value is null), plus `salt` only when the row has one, with its keys sorted at every depth (by UTF-16 code unit) and written as JavaScript writes an object built in that order: keys that are array indices (a whole number from 0 to 4294967294 written plainly, such as `2` or `10`, not `02`) come first, by number, then the rest in sorted order, so `{"10":1,"2":2,"a":3}` is written `{"2":2,"10":1,"a":3}`; a value with no such keys, every signed record among them, is plainly sorted. That is `src/lib/canonical-json.ts`, one copy for the server's hashes and signatures and the browser's signature check; `scripts/verify-record.mjs` keeps its own, held to the same bytes by `tests/canonical-json.test.ts`. The salt is there because every other field of a sealed row is visible to someone (a webhook receiver sees the time, actor, action, event and target and the row's hash; `audit.csv` gives the hash before it), and a ballot or a review has few possible values: without it, hashing each candidate ballot or score finds the one the row holds. It is shown only where the values are: never in a webhook body; in `audit.csv` at once for the rows whose values the CSV shows, and for a ballot only once voting has closed (until then its `before`, `after` and `salt` cells all read "hidden until voting closes"). So each line of `audit.csv` whose values are shown can be checked on its own: its columns `at`, `actor_user_id`, `actor` (the label), `action`, `event_id`, `target_type`, `target_id`, `before`, `after` (a blank cell is null, the rest JSON) and `salt` (blank: no key) rebuild the object, and `sha256(prev_hash + "\n" + that JSON)` must equal `hash` (a text cell that starts with an apostrophe had one added, the CSV's guard against spreadsheet formulas, which it puts before `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab, a carriage return and a value's own leading apostrophe; drop that one apostrophe first). A row's `prev_hash` is the hash of the portal's row before it, which may belong to another event or to no event, so the CSV proves each row and, where rows follow each other, their order. Rows without a salt hash exactly as they did before the column existed, so a chain an older portal wrote verifies unchanged; it also means a ballot or a review saved before the upgrade stays as guessable from its hash as it was (a ballot's picks until voting closes), and only rows written after it are protected. Recomputing the chain from the first row (`verifyAuditChain`) shows any edited, dropped or reordered row: it breaks at that id. What it proves: the log, as stored, is the log that was written, in that order. What it does not: someone holding the database file can rewrite rows and recompute a consistent chain — the triggers only stop edits made through SQLite's SQL layer. Record the chain's head hash elsewhere (it is included in the CSV export) to make tampering detectable. The portal hands such heads out as it goes: every signed record carries, inside its signature, the number and hash of the newest entry when it was signed (its page says whether the log still holds that entry as signed), and the public results page shows the entry that published the results. Whoever holds a record or saved the page holds a copy of that hash, which a rewrite of the log up to that entry would change. Rows cut off the end are the one change the chain cannot show by itself, since what remains is still a whole chain. Two things catch a cut. The check counts ids: AUTOINCREMENT gives each audit row the next one (a rolled-back write gives its ids back) and keeps the highest it gave in `sqlite_sequence`, which nothing in the app lowers. So ids that skip, or a count past the last row, are rows removed outside the app (or written past by a tool), and the log pages and `audit.csv` say which, also after the app has written more rows. That is a tripwire for a careless cut, since whoever edits the file can renumber the rows and lower the count too. What catches them is a head kept outside the portal, and the portal hands those out: each signed record (the newest entry when it was signed), the public results page (the entry that published them), each webhook delivery (its row's id and hash, kept by the receiver), and every `audit.csv` (each line carries the whole log's head: `chain_head`, its hash, and `chain_head_entry`, its entry number, the pair a record pins). The log must still hold that entry with that hash, and a later export whose head entry is lower than a saved one's was cut. Rows written after the newest head anyone kept, then cut with the count lowered, cannot be detected.

**`fixture_imports`** — one row per import call; a start skips a file whose `sha256` is already here. `id`; `source`; `sha256` of the imported file; `imported_at`; `counts` json (rows inserted per table).

## Rate limits

**`rate_buckets`** — one token bucket of the rate limits (`src/server/rate-limit.ts`), kept here so a restart keeps it and every process on the same file shares it. `key` pk: the limit's name and an HMAC-SHA256 of the whole key under `DOGFOOD_SEED_SECRET`, e.g. `signin:<64 hex>` for the key `signin:<email>:<network address>` (`storedKey`), so the same person and address always count on the same row but the table holds no email or address; `tokens` real (CHECK: never below 0); `at` (milliseconds since 1970, when `tokens` was last worked out); `refused` (the last take was refused, so the next refusal is not audited again). A key with no row has a full bucket; rows idle for longer than the slowest refill (an hour) are deleted as limits are taken and at every start, which also deletes any row an older portal wrote with its key in the clear.

## Relationships

- Event 1—n tracks, prizes, custom questions, project field choices, rubric criteria, teams, projects, assignments, votes, comments, signed records, webhooks.
- User n—m event through `user_roles` (one row per role); a person can be organizer, judge and participant at once.
- Team n—m users through `team_members`, one team per person per event; projects belong to one team and one track of the same event (composite foreign keys).
- Assignment run 1—n assignments; assignment 1—1 score; score 1—n score items and 0—1 score comment.
- Judge 1—n comparisons; a comparison names two projects of one track of the same event (composite foreign keys).
- Normalization run 1—n normalized scores; publishing an event stores which run is published in `events.settings`.
- Voter 1—n votes; voter is a user, a listed email, or a link holder.
- Signing key 1—n signed records; webhook 1—n deliveries; delivery references the audit row it came from.
- Event 1—n outbox messages; a message with no event is the portal's own.

## Where the fixture lands

The import (`src/server/db/import-fixtures.ts`) is idempotent — every insert is INSERT OR IGNORE, and a start does not import the same file twice, so the organizers' later edits and removals survive the next boot — and non-destructive: unknown or conflicting rows are skipped and reported, never thrown away. A later file that brings the missing scores of a review an earlier import brought in part finishes that review as a judge's save would: its assignment turns `done` and its score gets its `submitted_at` (a recused assignment stays recused).

|---|---|
| `event` | `events` (slug derived from the name, `submissions_close_at` kept exactly as given; a new event also takes the portal's added `submissions_open`, `judging_close`, `voting_open`, `voting_close`) |
| `tracks` | `tracks` |
| (score criteria keys, first-seen order) | `rubric_criteria` (weight 1, scale 1–5, built-in prompts where known) |
| `judges` | `users` (no password), `user_roles` (judge), `judge_tracks` |
| `teams` | `teams` (fresh random `invite_code`) |
| `teams[].members` | `users` (unless the email is already a judge's or has an account — one person, one account), `user_roles` (participant), `team_members` (first member is captain) |
| `projects` | `projects` (status `submitted`, both rows of a duplicate pair kept; in a new event a project's `duplicate_of` is the organizer's merge, one level, both copies in the file) |
| `scores` | one `assignment_runs` row (`run_fixture_<event>`, mode `fixture`), then `assignments` (done when complete), `scores` (with `conflicted` where the judge is on the scored team), `score_items` (missing or null keys are not scored, never zeros), `score_comments` (`comment` is the feedback, `private_note` the note to the organizers); a new event's review is finished at its `submitted_at`, one brought into an event that is here at the import |

| `comparisons` | a new event's `comparisons`, taken-back ones too, each between two of the file's projects in the track it names, which each of the two is in now or was in before a move the file's `track_moves` record (an answer keeps its own track; the engine counts it only while both projects share a track, as on the old portal) |
| `ballots` | a new event's `voters` (an account by its address, a listed address, an open-link voter by nothing; a listed or link voter gets a new secret nobody holds, so the old portal's links open nothing; the salted address and browser hashes that flag suspected duplicate voters stay behind) and `votes`; a set-aside ballot keeps its reason. While voting is open the export seals the picks as `audit.csv` does: it carries only the listed addresses (one set aside still set aside, with its reason and time) and `ballots_sealed`, the number left behind, which the report's `skipped` repeats |
| `comments` | a new event's `comments`, by their author's address (an account found or made as a member's is), a hidden one still hidden with its reason |
| `published` | a new event's `normalization_runs` row and `normalized_scores` as stored when published (never worked out again), then `results_published_at` and the run in `settings.publishedRunId`, last, since the database then freezes what the ranking rests on; as publishing ends a vote, a published file whose `voting_close` is later than `published.at` is a 422 |
| (the call itself) | `fixture_imports`, plus one `fixtures.import` audit row when anything was inserted: the file's SHA-256, the counts, and by name what judging rests on, each account the import made a judge of the event and each review it brought in or added to (judge, project, whether the review is finished once the import is done, which counts a review that an earlier file brought in part and this one completed, the scores and feedback it added), and each criterion it added to the rubric of an event that was already here; for a new event, `restored`: every prize, merge, decision, pairwise answer, ballot (by its voter and how many picks, never the picks), comment and published run it brought, by id. A webhook gets the counts only |

Imported rows keep the fixture's ids, with two exceptions listed in the report's `renamed`: a track, team or project id that another event already holds gets `.<event id>` appended, so a second event never links to the first one's rows (a file whose renamed id a third event holds too is refused whole, 409 `id_taken`), and a judge id that is another person's account gets an account of its own (`usr_` and a hash of the email). Assignments and scores get deterministic ids derived from the ids used (`asg_<judge>_<project>`, `scr_<judge>_<project>`), and a review handed out on the portal keeps its own and stays the judge's: the import finds the pair's assignment by judge and project, and when the portal handed it out (its run is not the event's `fixture` run) the import adds no score, value or feedback to it, whether it is pending, a draft or finished, and names the file's row in the report's `skipped`; an event's export brings those reviews back with the values they already hold, so it imports back unchanged; every imported review's assignment belongs to the `fixture` run, which is how `scores.csv` marks it `import` in its `source` column (a review given out on the portal reads `portal`). Through the API, a file for an event that already exists adds to it only for that event's organizers, and never once its results are published. It keeps the event's deadlines as the forms do, by the server's clock: no project once submissions have closed (409 `submissions_closed`), and no review, score or feedback once judging has closed (409 `judging_closed`), each naming the deadline, with nothing imported. It also keeps the rules the portal's forms keep, or is refused whole with a 409 naming the file's row: no team past the event's size (`team_full`), one project per team (`team_has_project`), and no review for a judge on the project's team, nor a member added to a team whose project they are assigned to judge (`conflict_of_interest`, from the database's memberships, not only the file's), and no member added to a team they have a community vote for (`vote_would_change`: the count leaves out a member's votes for their own team). A new event's file is the organizers' data as given: a team's two projects both come in, and a judge's review of their own team's project comes in flagged (`scores.conflicted`).

An event's history (the `ballots`, `comments`, `comparisons`, `decisions`, merges and `published` keys) comes only into a new event (`src/server/db/import-history.ts`). Into an event that is here, each row the event already holds by its id (or by the id an earlier import renamed it to) is present and counted in `existing`, and a file that would add any other is refused whole with 409 `new_event_only`, naming what it would add: an import never adds a ballot, a comment, a pairwise answer, a merge, a decision or a published ranking to an event running here, and never publishes one. The event's own dates, settings and prizes stand; the report's `skipped` says where the file differs. In a new event every restored row names only the file's own projects, tracks and judges: a pick, comment, answer, merge or published row that names anything else is skipped and listed, never linked to another event's row. Ids another event holds get `.<event id>` appended as tracks' do, and the published run's stored record follows every renamed id. The results page shows its audit seal only for results published on this portal; an imported event's publication is in the import's audit row.

## Privacy

Personal data stored: names and lowercased emails in `users` (and `judge_invites`, and `voters` for listed voters); argon2id password hashes in `users.password_hash`; who commented or voted (user ids); the body of comments. For duplicate-voter detection only, `voters.ip_hash` and `voters.agent_hash` hold SHA-256 hashes of the network address and user agent, salted with `DOGFOOD_SEED_SECRET` and the event id; the raw values are never stored, and the hashes are only compared with each other. The rate limits count people by email address, network address, account id or voter (sign-in tries per email and address, sign-ups and sign-ins per address, unknown voting codes per address, comments per account, refused requests per person); `rate_buckets.key` stores each as the limit's name and an HMAC-SHA256 under the same secret, never the email or address itself. The salt is only as private as the secret: with the documented default, someone holding the database could hash every IPv4 address and match them, so a real event sets its own secret (README, "Running it for a real event"). Every link-style credential is stored only as its unsalted SHA-256 (`sessions.token_hash`, `judge_invites.code_hash`, `voters.token_hash`, `api_tokens.token_hash`, `account_claims.token_hash`), so a database dump cannot be used to sign in. Two secrets are kept because the server needs them to prove things to others, not to identify people: `signing_keys.private_pkcs8` (to sign records at request time) is sealed with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from `DOGFOOD_SEED_SECRET` and the key's id (`src/server/signing.ts`), so with your own `DOGFOOD_SEED_SECRET` a copy of the database alone cannot sign (the documented default opens it for anyone); `webhooks.secret` (to sign each delivery) is stored as it is. The audit log keeps actor ids and labels, and its `before`/`after` snapshots can contain names or emails — it is readable by organizers only, and exportable as CSV.

### What is kept, and for how long

The portal's own `/privacy` page ("What we keep") shows this same list; the sign-up form, the comment box, the voting-link page and the ballot each say in one line what they keep and link to it. Both come from `src/lib/privacy-facts.ts`, and `tests/privacy-facts.test.ts` fails when this table and that list differ.

| What | Where | Kept | Removed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| A person's name, email address and password hash | `users` | as long as the portal's data | nothing in the portal yet: removing the data volume |
| Who organizes, judges or takes part in which event, and which tracks a judge covers | `user_roles`, `judge_tracks` | as long as the portal's data, or until an organizer takes the person off the event | an organizer (removing a judge or an organizer); the audit log keeps the change |
| Team membership: who is on which team, and who is its captain | `team_members` | until the person leaves, is taken off the team, or the team is disbanded | the person, the team's captain, the organizers |
| The name and email address a judge was invited with | `judge_invites` | as long as the portal's data | removing the data volume |
| Judging: the projects a judge was given, their scores per criterion, their feedback to the team, their private notes to the organizers and their pairwise answers | `assignments`, `scores`, `score_items`, `score_comments`, `comparisons` | as long as the portal's data, and once the event's results are published the database refuses to change or delete them | nothing in the portal: a started review stays in the record (before publication the judge can change it; an organizer can withdraw only a review not yet started) |
| Which organizer made an assignment run, a results run, a judge override (with its reason) or a webhook | `assignment_runs`, `normalization_runs`, `judge_overrides`, `webhooks` | as long as the portal's data; results runs for good (append-only) | removing the data volume |
| Email addresses on an organizer's voter list | `voters.email` | as long as the portal's data | removing the data volume |
| Comments, with the author's name beside them | `comments` | until the author deletes it (not while the organizers have it hidden) | the author |
| Community-vote picks | `votes`, `voters` | as long as the portal's data | the voter, while voting is open |
| Keyed hashes of a voter's network address and browser | `voters.ip_hash`, `voters.agent_hash` | until the operator purges them, once the vote has closed | `scripts/purge.mjs` |
| Sign-in sessions (stored as a hash of the cookie) | `sessions` | until sign-out or their end (`SESSION_DAYS`, default 14); an ended one is deleted within the hour | the portal, by itself |
| A person's named API tokens (stored as a hash), with their first characters and when they were last used | `api_tokens` | as long as the portal's data: a revoked or expired token stays as a row that no longer works | removing the data volume (the person can revoke one, which does not remove it) |
| Password-reset and account-claim links (stored as a hash): for whom, who made them, when they were used | `password_resets`, `account_claims` | as long as the portal's data: a used or expired link stays as a row; an unused one is replaced by the next | nothing in the portal (neither the hourly sweep nor `scripts/purge.mjs`): removing the data volume |
| Keyed hashes of an email or network address, counting tries | `rate_buckets` | about an hour after the last try (idle buckets are deleted every hour) | the portal, by itself |
| Mail the portal sent: address, subject, body with its link blanked (nothing while email is off) | `outbox` | until the operator purges it (older than 90 days by default) | `scripts/purge.mjs` |
| Changes sent to the organizers' webhooks, which can include names, and the receivers' answers | `webhook_deliveries` | until the operator purges the finished ones (older than 90 days by default) | `scripts/purge.mjs`; the receiver keeps its own copy |
| Signed certificates, with the person's name | `signed_records` | for good: whoever holds one can check it; its page asks search engines not to index it | nothing |
| Project pictures and gallery images, re-encoded without their metadata | the uploads folder | until the team replaces or removes it, or the organizers take it down | the team, the organizers |
| The audit log: who did what and when, by name, and email addresses in some entries (a judge invited, an organizer added or removed) | `audit_log` | for good: it is append-only, and removing a row would break the chain | nothing |
| Backups: a copy of all of the above | `/data/backups` | the newest 7 (`BACKUP_KEEP`) on the volume, and any copied off it | the operator |

The portal itself deletes ended sign-in sessions and idle rate-limit buckets at every start and every hour (`src/server/retention.ts`). An operator removes the rest of what the table marks with `scripts/purge.mjs` (`docker compose exec portal node scripts/purge.mjs` shows what it would remove; `--yes` removes it; `--days N` sets the age, default 90): old mail, finished webhook deliveries, and the address hashes of every event whose vote has closed (the count does not change; the duplicate flags beside it go). It never touches the audit log, scores, ballots, comments or accounts, and it writes no audit row, since it works on the file outside the portal. Removing a person's account, or their name from the audit log, is not built: today only removing the whole data volume (`docker compose down -v`, after exporting what should stay) erases them. The audit log's names are frozen by its hash chain.
